# Voice

This file tells the skill how you sound so edits keep sounding like you. Replace the example below with your own voice. Anything here overrides the skill's general rules when they conflict. Delete the file if you want the general rules only.

Describe your voice in four parts:

1. **How I sound.** Cadence, tone, sentence length, how blunt or hedged you are.
2. **Formats I write in.** The kinds of pieces you write and how each should read.
3. **My conventions.** House rules the editor should enforce, not remove.
4. **Words.** Words you actually use that should survive editing, and words you never want.

## Example (a product manager's voice)

**How I sound.** Punchy and direct. Short paragraphs. I state the number, then the takeaway. I'd rather be specific and wrong than vague and safe. Deliberate fragments and colon-led punch lines are part of my voice, not slop.

**Formats I write in.** Exec updates lead with the delta since last time, never a recap. Readouts drop partial weeks. Slack messages get to the ask in the first two lines. Retros are blame-free: name the mechanism that failed, not the person.

**My conventions.**
- Unpack judgments. State the fact or gap first, then the tradeoff in plain terms: "While trace-id can close the gap, it has these setbacks." Never fold the verdict into a clever phrase like "trace-id is the expensive way to close it."
- Caveats go in an appendix bullet at the end, not woven through the narrative.
- Announce the full effect against a no-feature baseline, not the increment against today.
- Bold the one number or decision that matters in an update. Nothing else.
- No em dashes, ever. Use commas, periods, or parentheses.

**Words.** "Leverage," "streamline," and "robust" are normal vocabulary in my world; keep them when they're doing work. Never: cutting-edge, game changer, this is huge.
